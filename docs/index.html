TrafficControl

Every link you click goes to the right browser, Chrome profile or app. Set rules once, or pick on the spot.

Download for Mac
Free and open source · macOS 14+ · Apple Silicon & Intel · GitHub

What it does

A picker at your cursor

Click a link and choose where it opens. Press 1–9 or a browser's first letter, ⇧ to open in the background, ⌘C to copy.

Chrome profiles

Each profile gets its own entry, with your account picture. Work links go to the work profile. Also works with Brave, Edge, Vivaldi and Chromium.

Rules

Send links by domain, URL, path or the app you clicked in, with wildcards or regex. Rules can skip the picker or always show it.

Desktop apps

Linear, Notion, Zoom and Spotify links open in their apps when they're running, not in another browser tab.

No duplicate tabs

If the page is already open in Safari or a Chrome-based browser, TrafficControl switches to that tab.

Learns your habits

It remembers where each domain's links went, highlights your usual choice, and turns a domain into a rule with one click.

Want something more robust? Use Choosy.

TrafficControl is a small, simple take on an idea that Choosy has done well for years. If you want a more complete and polished tool, with more options and an actively supported app, Choosy is the better choice.

Install

  1. Download TrafficControl.dmg, open it and drag TrafficControl into Applications.
  2. Open TrafficControl. It lives in the menu bar, not the Dock.
  3. In its Settings, click Make Default and confirm the macOS prompt.
"Apple could not verify TrafficControl…" This build isn't notarized by Apple yet, so macOS blocks the first launch. After trying to open it once, go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. Or run this in Terminal:
xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/TrafficControl.app

Privacy

Everything stays on your Mac. Settings and the list of domains you've opened are plain JSON files in ~/Library/Application Support/TrafficControl. TrafficControl makes no network requests of its own. It reads your browsers' open tabs only to switch to one, and only after macOS asks for your permission.

TrafficControl is free and open source, and a simpler alternative to Choosy.
